## 1. Users as creators

We may also be stepping into an era where part of being a creator is not just creating a final product, but creating a product through which users can express themselves. This is very powerful because customization, personalization are very strong things that can be leveraged into communities and meaning and belonging. I think that's a bit of a different argument when we start talking about personalization. But we are essentially moving into an area where the barrier to create something is a behavior that a user actually wants to engage in, as opposed to it being a chore. For example, Minecraft is effectively a building platform, but it only works because people actually like building in it. That is the product. So you are effectively making a tool. It's a bit of a blurred edge there. You're making a tool that is a product. And so the product itself is the tool. If you can make something like that people actually like, it's pretty powerful because then people start generating content with your tool. And they actually can take ownership and build community around it. And they have authorship. So they're not showing up as a consumer, they're showing up as a creator. That is quite engaging on another level. Often this starts with a smaller demographic, which are obsessed around building or things like that. But they're the ones who effectively evangelize because they make incredible things. Other people see it and they say, I also want to join in and make cool things like them.
