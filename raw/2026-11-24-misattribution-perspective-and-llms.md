# Misattribution, perspective and LLMs

**Date:** 2026-11-24
**Source:** dictated, project chat
**Topics:** topics/ai-jagged-displacement-in-product-making.md, topics/restraint-taste-and-discernment.md

---

Well, the key thing is because the qualitative stuff is so hard to measure, we see where players put their money. But because we can't measure these things, we misattribute why those things are successful. When was the last time you heard someone say, this game is good because the gun shooting feels great? Only people who really understand that stuff can tell you that more specifically. Most people will just tell you, it feels good. It feels smooth. They'll give you some kind of nebulous answer like that. But I guess that's true with any of the aspects of a game. Like, even at the high level, like the designs being cool, what does that mean, right? Why is Elden Ring's design so cool and others are not so cool? People probably can't articulate. They just know it looks cool, and they know that it's differentiated, and they know because it feels like they're in a place that is specific. That specificity comes from a perspective, or a collective group of people that are really honing in on a perspective. And this comes down to a fundamental problem with LLMs, which is they codify language. And so much of what we like about games when it comes to the look of something and the details and the feel and all these things are often not things that we can articulate. We are getting to the point where sometimes an AI can view what's been done in another game through a video and then derive the logic that arrives at that game feel, for example. But if you just pull out a design and you ask an LLM to identify why a design is good, ultimately, it doesn't have a perspective to really do that by. Perspectives, like taste, is not about just logically solving for something. It's about saying, I like this and I don't like that. And whether something succeeds or not depends on whether other people agree that they like that more than something else.
