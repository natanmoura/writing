## 3. Where ideas come from: the genius, individuality, and process under markets

I also want to talk about the idea of where ideas even come from. And what is going on with that? You know, back in the day, the Greeks used to talk about ideas as being disembodied things, things that essentially were floating in the ether. You would grab a hold of and you would sort of manifest into reality, right? You have your kind of spidey senses out and you're kind of grabbing these things out of the ether, right? And that's what we started calling a genius in the first place, was exactly that. The genius was effectively a type of alien experience where you'd be abducted by this entity called the genius, and then it would act through you and create some phenomenal thing. And so in a sense, they were essentially saying that their own authored self was not actually part of the, it was not actually the thing that was acting. There was something more divine, more spiritual that was acting through them. Now, it is important that we're saying that something is acting through something, right? It's not just the genius was doing it all. The genius was acting through you. So you are part of that process. But somewhere down the line, we became very, very very, very connected to this idea of our identity, our individuality, and our ego. Essentially, that perspective of ours being the actual source of creative acts and creative vision, right? I would argue that this is actually potentially a more modern, postmodern, and modern sensibility and way of viewing the world. I think in some sense, AI is challenging this because AI is, showing a kind of creativity that can take place that is sort of remixing, it's sort of synthesizing a lot of different things and creating something new. And the people who are stuck in this paradigm that everything is actually individual authoring, that is the only possible source for creativity, are in some sense in my opinion, I think maybe are getting a little caught up in their identity being central to the creative act, which it can be, but it also doesn't have to be. I often see kids when they play, they have their identity that is involved in the process, but actually there is a freedom that is sort of, I think, beyond the identity, that is actually coming from a sense of openness to things around that may not actually even come from them, and just reacting to it and remixing those things. And I think AI actually provides some opportunities around this that we are kind of undervaluing because we overvalue individuality.
