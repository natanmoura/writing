# Automation in the differentiating layer

**Date:** 2026-11-24
**Source:** dictated, project chat
**Topic:** topics/ai-jagged-displacement-in-product-making.md

---

You need to find the layer in what you're building that is the differentiator . You don't need to protect it from automation, but if you are going to use automation for it, you need to make sure that it is at the actual quality that is extremely high. If you can use automation tools to arrive at really, really good game feel, then that is great. Of course, it is also true that past a certain point, there may be diminishing returns. It's also true that some games don't win on a single thing. Some. Some games are product win on an entire ecosystem of things that aren't necessarily all that differentiated or polished, but the sum of all their parts actually creates something that feels that is differentiated as a whole. As opposed to by individual parts. you need to have the discernment to understand what is actually differentiating what you do from what anyone else is doing. If you want to cut corners, no one is stopping you. But again, it comes down to your discernment . A lot of people will cut corners . Their product won't work and they will misattribute that to some other reason other than the fact that the quality simply was not there.. The reason being they will not understand what they are missing qualitatively because because It is a blind spot. A lot of people do not know what they don't know. They'll often get an AI to search up what they don't know, and they'll implement that. But if you are doing that in an area where you're trying to differentiate, you are not actually going to end up differentiating a whole lot. It only maybe works in areas where you're only looking for good enough. But I have found that even people who think something is good enough often produce stuff that is far from good enough, and not in a way that only experts understand, but in a way that the market also immediately clocks.
