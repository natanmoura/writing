## 1. The leisure slot and niching out of it

Now, I also want to start on another new writing prompt, which is talking about how we it's like even higher level. It's like, how do we build products in this age? And specifically talking about things like apps and games and digital products, or any product for that matter. Actually, it's not just digital. We are trying to create something fundamentally that fits into someone's life. What that means is that if you are competing in a category where a lot of other things are already competing for that time in someone's life, it is actually an uphill battle. If you are trying to make entertainment like games, and movies, you are effectively competing with all the other stuff that's out there. Games are not just competing, and all of that is competing with each other, right? So for example, the decisions that someone makes in their day are often things like, am I going to play a game . Am I going to watch a movie, or am I going to go watch something on YouTube? All the videos on YouTube, all the games and all the movies are competing, usually on a very similar slot. So is doom scrolling. Because doom scrolling is effectively a slot machine. It's actually quite hard to get people to go do your thing instead. The reason why certain really big games succeed and the winners take the bigger slice of the pie is that people just have only so many hours in a day to play something. So they are going to go play not only the best quality thing, they're going to play the thing that other people are playing. Because we are fundamentally social creatures. That is why we get everyone converging on certain games and certain releases because this is a tribal thing. We want to be a part of the thing that everyone else is being a part of. It's a type of a social validation. But there are other categories that actually step out of areas that are under high competition and I would encourage people to think about more ideas in those categories. For example, if you make something in a category that solves a specific pain point for a specific group of person, you are essentially niching down into an area where other people simply are not. For example, if I make an app that helps me learn sign language, it is a much smaller demographic. But there might still be another demographic where actually that person learning sign language is important enough to them that they will be doing that outside of the window where they would be watching Netflix, YouTube, or playing a game.

## 2. Hardware: category, not moat

A lot of people are moving into hardware products because they believe there is more friction and higher difficulty on people competing on those vectors. But I actually think the benefit of competing on hardware is not specific to the moat. It's actually specific to the category. Hardware products like an alarm clock, a little Tamagotchi toy, or whatever, are not things people buy instead of games. It's something someone would buy on top of the games they would play. In other words, it is not competing on other categories. So if you can define hardware in a category of entertainment, there's a lot of people who will buy that before they would buy the game you were going to make, simply because they understand that a little Tamagotchi toy they put on their bag is a different value proposition than a game they're going to sit down and play in the evening that is competing with the YouTube and Netflix time slot. If you think about it, a little Tamagotchi toy you put on your bag competes in ways that other products don't. One, they sell to potentially a younger demographic that has more time off screens and is running around the schoolyard talking to friends and probably sharing these devices and these experiences together. It's something that is with you on the go in a way that other things are not. It's something you can play, show off outside in the world. This is a very different value proposition than something like a game, a movie, or YouTube, or doom scrolling. While the unit economics may be bad in hardware, there's a lot of opportunity there, which I think is more to do with the category and how it fits into your day. We use our phones as a device that we interface with while we're out in the world, but there are certain things that we might want to interface with, other objects that are simply just cool. They provide value, they do solve a problem, but they are fundamentally something different than a phone. They provide a different functionality, a different experience, and they provide it in a place where other things are not. And that's what you want to do at the end of the day. You want to show up in a place where other things are not, and then deliver something that people actually care about.
