# Working title: What AI can't keep

Alternates: "AI isn't the muse" / "Complementary, not antithetical" / "The ladder and the ether"

**From:** topics/ai-and-human-art.md (raw: raw/2026-11-26-ai-and-human-art-complementary.md)
**Status:** proposed

## Angle
Most takes on AI and art are either "AI is theft and the end of art" or "it's just a tool." Natan's dump makes a third case: AI doesn't threaten creativity, but it does threaten the conditions that make human creativity distinctive. Those are livelihoods, credit, and above all the passing on of tacit craft. The original part is that the knowledge AI can't encode is the same knowledge AI removes the incentive to pass on.

## Thesis (Claude's restatement, to be rewritten by Natan)
AI and human-made art are complementary. Cheap results push us to re-value process and perspective, and creativity will absorb AI the way it absorbed every other tool. But AI keeps only what has been written down. The tacit layer of craft, the feel and edge cases that make work specific, survives only through apprenticeship, and AI removes the market reason to apprentice. So the danger isn't that AI replaces art. It's that we lose the knowledge that made human art distinctive, faster than history has lost things before.

## Core argument
1. The either/or framing is wrong. AI makes results cheap, which re-values process, time and attention.
2. Perspective is built over time and is visible across a body of work, not in one piece. AI produces pieces.
3. Authorship is the decisions you make. One-shotting is a lottery, and staying in the loop is where expression lives (synthesizer, drawing tool).
4. AI encodes what has been recorded. The specific feel and in-the-moment discoveries usually aren't recorded, so AI only reproduces the common, derivative layer.
5. That tacit layer is passed on by apprenticeship, which depends on markets. AI removes the incentive, and juniors stop entering the field.
6. Knowledge has always been lost and rediscovered (the Renaissance), but rediscovery needed keepers, and the cycle now runs at the speed of technology.
7. So the real threats are to livelihoods, to credit and to the passing on of craft, not to creativity. What to do: choose what you spend your time on, credit sources, and build apprenticeships deliberately.
