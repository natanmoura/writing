# Working title: Beyond utility

Alternates: "Nobody buys $5,000 sunglasses for the sunglasses" / "Who decides what it's worth"

**From:** topics/high-ticket-and-signaling.md
**Status:** early

## Thesis (draft)
Price something far enough above its category and it stops being that product. It sells story, craft and signal. Value is set by tastemakers and third-party validation, and longevity turns that perception into trust. When something isn't priced on utility, the ceiling is how good the story is, and whether there's a real, differentiated way of making it underneath.

## Possible structure
1. The category ceiling, and what happens when you break it (sunglasses, watches)
2. Collections: when value is perception
3. Tastemakers and why we trust evangelists who didn't make the thing
4. Hype vs. longevity: what lasts
5. Art as the extreme case
6. (Strongest angle) Why this matters more in the AI age: provenance and perspective as the scarce vector
