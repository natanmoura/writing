# The product as a creative tool

**Source:** raw/2026-11-24-product-as-creative-tool.md
**Related:** topics/fitting-into-someones-life.md, topics/jagged-differentiation.md, topics/high-ticket-and-signaling.md, topics/ai-and-human-art.md

## Core idea
Part of being a creator now may be building a product through which *users* express themselves. When the act of creating is something people enjoy rather than a chore, the tool becomes the product (Minecraft). Users show up as creators instead of consumers, take ownership, build community, and the obsessed builders evangelize by making things others want to make too.

## Key points

### The tool is the product
- "Part of being a creator is not just creating a final product, but creating a product through which users can express themselves."
- Customization and personalization "can be leveraged into communities and meaning and belonging." (Natan flags personalization as "a bit of a different argument.")
- "The barrier to create something is a behavior that a user actually wants to engage in, as opposed to it being a chore."
- Minecraft "is effectively a building platform, but it only works because people actually like building in it. That is the product." "The product itself is the tool."

### Authorship and community
- Users generate content, "take ownership and build community around it," and "have authorship. So they're not showing up as a consumer, they're showing up as a creator."
- It usually starts with "a smaller demographic, which are obsessed around building." They evangelize "because they make incredible things," and others want to join in.

## Examples and anecdotes
- Minecraft (building as the product).
- Counterexample raised in chat: *Dreams*, acclaimed as a creation tool but commercially weak.
- Needed: what separated the creator platforms that took off from the ones that didn't.

## Tensions and contradictions
- **Evangelists vs. "we trust people who didn't make it"** (high-ticket topic). Here the evangelists *are* the makers, of their own creations. They're trusted because they're users, not the company. Worth stating how the two ideas fit.

## Open questions
- What makes creating feel like play instead of a chore? Is that a design problem, or a property of the medium?
- How does the product make money from the many people who don't create?
- How does AI change this? If AI does the making, what's left for the user to enjoy?
